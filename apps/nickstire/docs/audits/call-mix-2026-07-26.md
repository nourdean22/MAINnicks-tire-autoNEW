# VAPI call-mix measurement — 2026-07-26

**Method:** read-only aggregate SELECTs against production `vapi_call_logs`, main-loop,
no PII emitted (counts and percentages only). Window: 90 days (the table's full history —
first row 2026-05-20, so 90d == all rows).

**Why:** the repo's "~60% of inbound calls are used-tire inquiries" drove the entire
tire-first VAPI architecture, but it lived in a May-2026 source comment, not a demand
report. This was an attempt to verify it.

---

## Verdict on the ~60% claim: **UNVERIFIABLE with current instrumentation**

Not confirmed, not refuted. The two available signals cannot answer the question:

| Signal | Value | Why it cannot settle the claim |
|---|---|---|
| `serviceMention` | `tire` 1365 · `brake` 198 · **nothing else** | The field is effectively **binary**. 1365 + 198 = 1563 = every substantive call. Anything not classified brake becomes "tire", so "87% tire" measures the classifier's label set, not customer demand. |
| Keyword over `aiSummary` | "tire (any)" 97.4% | `aiSummary` is written by a **tire-first assistant**. It says "tire" in nearly every summary regardless of what the caller wanted. This measures the assistant's vocabulary, not the customer's need. |

**Explicit `used tire` mention: 213 / 1563 = 13.6%.** Treat as a *floor*, not an estimate —
summaries need not use the word "used". It is well below 60%, but the instrument is too
weak to call the claim refuted.

**To actually measure it** you need customer-turn classification over transcripts, not
summary keywords. `transcriptUrl` exists on the table; that is the path.

---

### The channel-priority finding

**Voice carries ~100× the inbound volume of SMS.**

- Voice: **2,094 calls / 90 days**
- SMS: **58 customer turns / 180 days** (ROS-058 audit, 2026-07-25)

Any conversation-quality work that targets SMS first is optimising the smaller channel by
two orders of magnitude. This is the strongest prioritisation signal in the dataset and it
argues for voice-first work — which also matches where the 40.3% forward rate sits.

---

## Honest limits of this measurement

- **One table, no transcripts.** Intent was inferred from a summary field, not customer speech.
- **`serviceMention` is not a taxonomy.** Two labels total; it cannot express "logistics",
  "status", "financing" or "complaint", so those are invisible here.
- **No inbound/outbound split** was applied; the table may include outbound calls.
- **No dedupe by caller.** A repeat caller counts once per call.
- **90d == all history.** No trend baseline exists before 2026-05-20.
- Percentages are of the ≥20s denominator (1,563) unless stated.

## Recommended next step

Do **not** re-anchor the architecture on any percentage from this table. Either:

1. Classify customer turns from `transcriptUrl` (real answer, more work), or
2. Widen `serviceMention` into a real label set going forward and re-measure in 30 days.

Until then the tire-first design should be treated as an **unverified working hypothesis**
(as now marked in `server/services/vapi.ts`), not an evidence-backed decision.

# ADDENDUM — customer speech is now stored, and the backfill window is HARD-CAPPED

## VAPI retains only 14 days on the current plan

Backfilling historical calls from `api.vapi.ai/call/{id}` returned:

```
HTTP 400 {"message":"Your subscription plan only covers the last 14 days of
call history. This call exceeds ..."}
```

for every call older than ~14 days — 1,197 of 1,600 attempts. This is **not**
rate limiting and no retry fixes it.

| | |
|---|---|
| Backfilled | **363 calls** (2026-07-12 → 2026-07-26) |
| Substantive calls in that window | 364 |
| **Coverage of the recoverable window** | **99.7%** |
| Recoverable beyond 14 days | **0 — permanently lost** |

The 363 are therefore not a random subset: they are essentially *the entire
retrievable corpus*, and a coherent recent window, which is good for describing
CURRENT demand and useless for trend.

## This makes deploying the webhook capture time-sensitive

Every day the `metadata.customerSpeech` webhook change sits unmerged, one more
day of transcripts crosses the 14-day line and becomes unrecoverable at any
price. After deploy the retention limit stops mattering — capture happens at
end-of-call, before expiry. Before deploy, the loss is silent and permanent.

That is the strongest argument for merging this PR promptly, independent of
anything else in it.

## What the recovered speech says (n=363, extractor v2)

Keyword classification over the caller's OWN first substantive turn still leaves
**~46% unmatched**, so this is NOT a demand answer and must not be quoted as
one. It is, however, direct evidence that keyword rules are insufficient and a
calibrated classifier (gold sample + per-class F1) is required.

One signal is stable across two independent passes and worth acting on:

| Signal | Pass 1 | Pass 2 (v2) |
|---|---|---|
| **`human_requested` as the OPENING request** | 11.6% | **12.1%** |

Roughly **1 in 8 callers asks for a person before the assistant has done
anything**. Against the 40.3% `assistant-forwarded-call` rate, that reframes a
large share of transfers as CUSTOMER_REQUESTED_REQUIRED — a success, not an AI
failure. Any "reduce transfers" target must exclude this class.

Directional only: tire-related ~21%, `used_tire` ~3%. Consistent with the other
two instruments in pointing far below 60%, but treat only the REFUTATION as
established, not these figures.

## Extractor bugs the real corpus exposed (v1 → v2)

Real data broke the instrument, which is why it was verified before being built
on:

- `"Hello? Hello?"` — the filler test anchored a SINGLE token, so a repeated
  greeting became the caller's "request". It is the most common opener in the
  corpus (callers greet twice while the assistant connects).
- The literal string `"null"` was stored as a customer turn.

Both fixed; version bumped to 2 so v1/v2 stay distinguishable. The 363 rows were
re-derived **from stored turns with no VAPI refetch** — which is precisely why
the turns are persisted and not just the derived field.

# ADDENDUM 2 — transfer compliance measured, and a CORRECTION to the 12.1% figure

## Correction: opening human-requests are ~21%, not 12.1%

The earlier 11.6–12.1% figure was an **undercount**. That rule required
`speak/talk TO (someone|a person)` and so missed the most common real phrasings:
`speak WITH a manager`, `customer service`, `just transfer me`, `connect me`,
and bare `Manager, please.`

Corrected, conservative rule (unambiguous openings only):

| | |
|---|---|
| Calls opening with a human request | **76 of 363 = 20.9%** |

**Roughly 1 caller in 5 asks for a person as their FIRST substantive utterance**,
before the assistant has done anything.

## Critical Rule #6 is working — 93.9% compliance

The VAPI prompt already mandates transferring on the caller's *first* ask, with
no "what's it about?" and no pitch. Measured against outcomes:

| Outcome of an opening human-request call | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| `assistant-forwarded-call` | 72 | **94.7%** |
| `customer-ended-call` | 4 | 5.3% |
| other / none | 0 | 0% |

Restricted to shop hours, when a live transfer is the expected behaviour
(Mon–Sat 8–18, Sun 9–16 ET):

| | |
|---|---|
| Opening human-requests during open hours | 49 |
| Forwarded | 46 |
| **Compliance** | **93.9%** |

**Median customer turns before the forward: 2.** The assistant is transferring
promptly, not qualifying or pitching first — i.e. Rule #6 is honoured in
practice, not just written down.

## This resolves most of the 40.3% transfer rate

About **21 percentage points** of calls open with an explicit human request, and
~95% of those forward. So a large share of the 40.3% `assistant-forwarded-call`
rate is `CUSTOMER_REQUESTED_REQUIRED` — the customer asked for a person and got
one. That is a **success**, and any "reduce transfers" target that counts it as
failure would be optimising against customers getting what they asked for.

## The only defect visible here: 3 open-hours misses

All three were `customer-ended-call` — the caller hung up rather than the
assistant refusing:

```
[customer-ended-call 27s, 3 turns]  "Customer service."
[customer-ended-call 20s, 2 turns]  "Can I speak with a manager, please?"
[customer-ended-call 49s, 4 turns]  "Manager, please."
```

**Hypothesis, NOT established:** the mandatory PHONE-CAPTURE-BEFORE-TRANSFER
step ("best number in case we get disconnected?") adds a turn between the ask
and the transfer, and some callers hang up during it. The turn counts (2–4) and
short durations are consistent with that, but n=3 is far too small to conclude,
and the prompt already says to transfer anyway if the caller pushes back. Worth
watching as volume accumulates; not worth changing on this evidence.
